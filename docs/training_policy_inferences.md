# Downstream training policy inferences

These are our coaching conclusions and numerical scenarios, not copied
calendars, fitted physiological thresholds, or guarantees of race readiness.
Private source identities, original materials, extracted prescriptions, and
research databases are excluded from the repository. Runtime policy does not
depend on those files.

## Volume and frequency

Higher sustained volume generally calls for more running opportunities, but
there is no established universal mileage breakpoint that determines the
optimal number of days. Frequency and volume describe related coaching choices;
their association does not show that adding days causes better adaptation.

Use an athlete-scaled range and a disclosed preference within it. Preserve
current availability, response and recovery checks. Running days and sessions
are different quantities; optional running, doubles and cross-training must not
be silently counted as required running days. A short support session must not
mechanically lower the athlete's ordinary-run reference.

## Long runs and supporting volume

A long run needs supporting easy volume. When the established long run is large
relative to sustained total volume, hold long-run progression while increasing
supporting volume. Do not shrink an issued long run solely to satisfy a newly
selected ratio, or infer a universal safe ratio from descriptive observations.

The general-fitness balance curve is a disclosed provisional coaching prior,
not a data-fitted law. Race preparation has its own completion-oriented
allocation; it must not demand extreme total volume merely because a general
fitness ratio was extrapolated. Quality-session mileage contributes to total
running volume, but its easy and demanding portions are not interchangeable
stimuli or recovery costs.

## Progression and recovery

Increase accepted targets gradually from a dated anchor, not whenever a rolling
window or planning horizon changes. Developing supporting easy volume can
precede further long-run growth. Confirm delivered exposure and current
responses before accepting another stage; projected completion is not observed
tolerance.

The existing session-distance guard and modeled fatigue remain separate checks.
Neither supplies a validated injury probability or physiological clearance.
Missed running, adverse responses and insufficient preparation can justify a
reassessment; an approaching event must not accelerate progression beyond the
accepted limits. More preparation time provides consolidation and interruption
room, not a quantified promise of reduced injury risk.

## Race preparation

Distance and date guide a pace-free coaching destination. Race preparation takes
priority over the separate general-fitness long-run goal, but must not erase
higher established volume or long-run exposure. These central completion
scenarios are explicit policy assumptions, not estimated minimum requirements:

| Distance | Long-run destination | Volume per seven days | Taper |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5K | 3.1 miles | 10 miles | 7 days |
| 10K | 5.5 miles | 14 miles | 7 days |
| Half marathon | 10 miles | 25 miles | 10 days |
| Marathon balanced or more preparation | 20 miles | 40 miles | 21 days |
| Marathon shorter preparation | 17 miles | 35 miles | 14 days |

Marathon preparation modes retain the existing conditional volume-growth
scenarios of 8%, 6% and 4% per seven-day review for shorter, balanced and more
preparation respectively. These are coaching assumptions, not fitted athlete
capacity. They do not replace observed session guards, supporting-volume
requirements, consolidation or whole-program feasibility checks.

Shorter-distance preparation does not automatically inherit those marathon
growth rates or a mandatory hard-quality quota. A short race can be approached
through easy running; faster and sustained work serve additional purposes when
appropriate. A training long run need not reach the full race distance, and the
event itself remains separate from a guarded training prescription. No 50K
guidance is authorized by these scenarios.

## Evidence limits

Incomplete durations or mileage remain unknown, not zero. Optional/ranged doses
stay ranges, and time-based work is not converted to distance using a guessed
pace. Related editions are not independent evidence. Descriptive coaching
patterns inform these broad choices but do not identify causal frequency
benefits, optimal weights, individual recovery times or injury thresholds.
